## Delivery and ownership

A concrete connection plan carries an optional typed infection payload on an operation whose completion effect is `InfectionDelivery`. The first kind is `PhantomLoad`, with a finite positive requested LOAD amount. The Connections scene sets the default to **8 LOAD** on `SimulationHost`; the Inspector value applies to newly generated DELIVERY plans and manual legacy threat profiles. The payload and parameter are fixed when the plan is queued. Existing delivery profiles receive the default payload while keeping their route, operation sequence, WORK, and requested LOAD. Newly generated DELIVERY purposes place the payload on their final operation.

Normal WORK completion emits the existing delivery occurrence and applies its payload to that connection's target node. `kill` before completion prevents both. Application happens once per operation; killing or retiring the carrier afterward leaves the node infected. The simulation host owns infection instances independently of connections and clears all of them on reset. An instance records its target, kind, amount, simulation application time, and source connection ID and operation index. One instance of a kind may be active on a node. A repeated application is rejected and recorded without stacking or refreshing the original.

The fixed-step clock is authoritative. There is no duration, automatic recovery, or direct Health damage from infection application. Player cleaning follows the PID-Select session above.

## Resource effect

For each target node, the allocator reserves authored baseline LOAD first, then grants infection demand from remaining capacity, then shares what remains proportionally among active logical connections. Requested LOAD is baseline plus infection requests plus connection requests. Allocated LOAD is the corresponding sum and never exceeds capacity. Phantom LOAD continues requesting resources even with no logical connections. For capacity 10, baseline 0, Phantom LOAD 8, and two 5-LOAD connections, each connection receives 1 LOAD and advances WORK at 1 unit per second. With baseline 2, the same infection receives 8 and those connections receive zero.

The existing node LOAD bar reads authoritative total requested LOAD. Ordinary inspect does not name the infection or reveal hidden intent. Legitimate connections slowed by the reduced allocation affect Network Health through its existing service rules. The infection is not a logical connection and does not directly penalize Health.

## Developer controls

Editor and development builds support `spawn <profileId>` as before. For a purpose template, `spawn <purposeId> obj:loader` forces LOADER and runs the existing high-LOAD anomaly recipe. `spawn <purposeId> obj:delivery inf:phantom-load` forces DELIVERY and uses the scene's default infection amount; append `infp:load=8` to force an explicit positive amount. `obj:delivery` also defaults to Phantom LOAD when `inf:` is omitted. Forced objectives skip the ambient hostile and delivery probability rolls, while normal purpose sampling and the selected anomaly recipe still use the seeded generator. Infection options require DELIVERY; `infp:load` requires `inf:phantom-load`. Invalid options, unknown purposes, and invalid amounts return errors before a plan is queued. The command creates a real queued connection; infection application waits for its operation to complete. The older fixed profile IDs retain their plain `spawn <profileId>` behavior.

For mechanical tests in Editor and development builds: `infect <nodeId> phantom-load [load]` injects directly, `uninfect <nodeId>` removes Phantom LOAD, and `infections` prints authoritative state. These are debug controls, distinct from player `clean` gameplay. Direct injection and removal use the same host state and allocator as delivered infections.

## Internal recorder evidence

`plan_queued.definition.operations` includes each concrete operation's infection kind and LOAD parameter. Purpose-generated plans retain their decision and anomaly trace; `generation.forcedOptions` records the explicit objective, infection type, and whether a LOAD amount was forced. Application, duplicate rejection, and debug removal emit structured `infection_applied`, `infection_rejected`, and `infection_removed` events with target, kind, amount, time, reason, and source provenance. Snapshots include active infection instances and per-node baseline, infection request/allocation, connection request/allocation, and totals. This truth remains in the internal run log rather than ordinary inspection. The additive fields keep schema version 1.

## Blackout and Spawner extension (PR #33 baseline, superseded where noted above)

Blackout makes a node unavailable immediately. After the current fixed step's completed operations and their effects are processed in order, the host resolves every still-active or pending connection whose authored route includes that node as `Blackout`. This includes transit routes. An already completed carrier resolves `Completed`; interrupted connections deliver no unfinished effects and incur no incorrect-kill penalty. A queued arrival with an offline route node is rejected at dispatch without a successful spawn or automatic reroute. The recorder keeps its full queued plan, an `arrival_rejected` event with route and blocked node, and a distinct interruption resolution. Node headings display `OFFLINE` as a service symptom without naming the infection in ordinary inspection.

Each blacked-out node causes the Network Health tuning's default **2 Health per simulation second** outage drain even when there are no connections. This joins ordinary degradation, suppresses recovery, and appears as `blackoutNodeCount` and `outageDrainRate` in Health snapshots. Removal stops future outage drain and restores route availability; reset clears the infection.

Spawner waits a finite positive cadence, default **20 simulation seconds**, before each attempt. Every attempt is recorded. A successful attempt queues one ordinary hostile DELIVERY connection for dispatch on the next fixed step. It starts at the infected node, chooses a different reachable internal target using existing physical routes and purpose templates, and carries a concrete Phantom LOAD, Blackout, or Spawner payload. Its ID, operations, LOAD, WORK, completion and kill behavior are ordinary logical-connection behavior. The parent and child remain independent: killing a child prevents its delivery; removing a parent stops future attempts but retains queued and active children. An offline origin or lack of an available internal route skips an attempt with a recorded reason. Child generation uses a separate seeded random stream from ambient purpose/anomaly generation; IDs retain the existing separate ID stream. `plan_queued` and `spawner_attempt` evidence links the parent node, source carrier, infection application time, child ID, route, and payload.

Generated DELIVERY purpose plans choose one of the three kinds with serialized nonnegative weights (1/1/1 initially), recording the concrete payload in the queued plan. A forced `obj:delivery` without `inf:` retains the earlier Phantom LOAD default. LOADER still carries no infection.

Development controls additionally accept `inf:blackout`, `inf:spawner`, and `infp:cadence=N` with a matching Spawner kind. `infect <node> blackout` and `infect <node> spawner [cadence]` inject directly; `uninfect <node> <kind>` removes the named kind. Bare `uninfect <node>` still removes Phantom LOAD. Different kinds may coexist on one node, while a duplicate of one kind is rejected. These controls remain internal mechanical tooling, not player cleaning or investigation.

The Infection Witness exercises interruption and rejected routes, outage Health, Spawner cadence and removal, child delivery of all three kinds, seeded replay, overlap/reset, and saved causal JSON. Interactive 16:9 Play Mode review remains necessary:

1. Inject `infect API blackout` during ordinary traffic. Confirm the node reads `OFFLINE`, affected paths depart, later arrivals are rejected, and Health falls without active connections.
2. Run `uninfect API blackout`; confirm the node returns to its role label, route arrivals resume, and outage drain stops.
3. Run `infect DB spawner 20`; watch a child originate at DB only after the cadence, inspect its normal operation, and kill it before delivery. Let the next child finish and confirm a persistent infection at its target.
4. Queue `spawn product-lookup obj:delivery inf:blackout`, then observe its completed carrier and any interrupted routes. Use `infections`, `mark`, and `log save` to compare the visible symptoms with the internal causal JSON. Check terminal focus, route/label crowding, and propagation under ambient traffic.

Throttle, infection investigation, scoring, and Packet integration remain outside this work. Further cleaning minigames and modifiers remain future design material.
