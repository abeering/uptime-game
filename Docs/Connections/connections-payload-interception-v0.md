# Connections — Payload & Interception V0

This is a Connections implementation. The entity named Payload here is distinct from the typed *infection payload* already carried by INFECTOR connections. Packet mechanics are unchanged.

## Playable experiment

`payload <targetNodeId> [steady|variable]` is a development-build command. It chooses a connected origin, creates a stable `xN` identity, and plans a physical route to the specified target. The target and complete route are retained internally but are not shown by ordinary player commands. V0 has no ambient Payload generation. A deterministic witness may specify an origin and per-hop timings directly.

Only the current hop appears. Its thin amber segment grows for 1.2 simulation seconds, with a socket at the next node. Then a circular Payload body with its `xN` ID centered inside appears and traverses the segment at a constant speed. Its current hop participates in the same route ordering, node-port packing, lane width, and animated reflow as ordinary connections; it has no separate identity pill. Ordinary identity pills still pack around its lane. At an intermediate node, that segment closes and the next begins establishing. Steady hop durations sample 15–20 simulation seconds; Variable samples 7–25 seconds. A separate seeded random stream keeps these samples reproducible without consuming ambient traffic rolls. At its final node the Payload attempts one Phantom LOAD application through the existing infection machinery. It consumes no LOAD or WORK while moving.

`intercept [payloadId] @ <nodeId>` activates an interceptor immediately for five simulation seconds. The original V0 required a Payload ID; the node-wide follow-up makes that filter optional. `intercept @ api` can arm before any Payload exists and catches every Payload attempting its final action at API during the window, including simultaneous arrivals and later arrivals after an earlier catch. `intercept x1 @ api` matches only the specified active Payload. Explicit unknown or resolved IDs are rejected. Both forms share one active slot per node and a 15-second cooldown measured from activation. Both timings and the single four-LOAD demand are serialized tuning fields on the scene's `PayloadDirector`. Catches do not extend the window or add another LOAD cost. A pass-through, an incorrect node, an arrival after expiry, or a different Payload under the targeted form cannot be intercepted. Incorrect node predictions are accepted without route validation. The slot remains occupied and consumes LOAD until its original expiry. At an exact shared arrival/expiry timestamp, arrival is evaluated first. Interception prevents each caught entity's final action before any effect is applied.

The allocator grants baseline LOAD first, existing infection demand second, defensive demand third, then shares the remainder proportionally among ordinary connection requests. This preserves the established baseline and infection priority. Interception remains reliable even if its allocated defensive LOAD is less than requested; allocation measures its operational cost, not success probability. It has no direct Health penalty. A legitimate connection can slow under the reduced capacity and affect Health through the existing connection-only rule.

The beneficial node-status family shows `INTERCEPT ALL` with a radial timer throughout a node-wide window, including after catches. Targeted defense shows `INTERCEPT xN`, or `CAUGHT xN` after a successful final-node catch. It shares the family panel with CLEAN when both are present. The status reads authoritative simulation state; the terminal confirms activation as `INTERCEPT ACTIVE ALL @ API` or the selected ID, and confirms every catch as `INTERCEPTED xN`. Internal run logs retain creation route and hop timings, movement transitions, final action, infection provenance, interceptor window/cooldown/outcome, defensive demand/allocation, and snapshots. Interceptor events include `interceptAllPayloads` and `interceptCaughtCount`; success events retain the actual caught `payloadId` and the original activation's `actionSequence`. Snapshots retain `allPayloads`, the optional filter `payloadId`, and `caughtCount`. The ordinary terminal does not reveal the planned target.

## Payload Strike Telegraphing V0 update

The earlier all-amber presentation now has two current-hop colors. Orange means the next node is transit and no infection is attempted there. Strong red means the next node is the final node and the Payload will attempt infection on arrival. Red begins with that hop's line establishment and colors its line, next-hop socket, moving circular body, and `xN` ID through traversal. A one-hop Payload is red from its first establishment. The color follows the authoritative route and hop (`NextNode == FinalNode`), including after an intermediate arrival and during lane reflow; it introduces no movement or interception rule. Red announces an **attempt**, not a guaranteed infection: an interceptor may catch it, or infection application may be rejected. Both colors are Inspector values on `PayloadDirector` in the Connections scenes.

## Manual Play Mode review

The original amber-only review instructions below remain as V0 provenance. For the current telegraph, review orange pass-through hops, red final-hop establishment and traversal, a one-hop red appearance, and simultaneous orange/red Payloads at 16:9.

In either Connections scene, use the development command `payload db variable`. Try `intercept @ db`, then separately `intercept xN @ db` using the returned ID. Activate shortly before the visible Payload reaches its final hop socket. With several Payloads approaching DB, check that broad defense confirms each catch and keeps `INTERCEPT ALL` and its timer visible; targeted defense catches only its selected ID. Check pre-arming an empty node, wrong-node predictions, expiry, cooldown feedback, and an invalid explicit ID. Verify that the amber line reveals only one hop, the ID stays inside the moving circle, the Payload and ordinary paths keep separate aligned lanes during reflow, the status timer and terminal feedback are legible at 16:9, the DB LOAD cost appears once for the entire window, and command input retains focus. Recheck ordinary logical connections and a moving body near an ordinary identity pill. Pill/body collision avoidance is left for this visual playtest. Earlier scripted Play Mode captures do not replace this interactive timing review.

`PayloadWitness` covers both command forms, malformed syntax, pre-arming, simultaneous and sequential catches, exact expiry, post-expiry delivery, one-slot/cooldown rules, targeted filtering, pass-through behavior, zero defensive allocation, status visibility, recorder JSON, and reset, alongside the existing motion, shared-lane, allocation, and Health cases.

This phase does not introduce Worms, isolation, new effects, ambient Payload spawning, or Packet changes. Further playtesting should assess whether the fixed five-second window and the initial speed ranges yield useful prediction decisions.
