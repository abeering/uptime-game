# Connections — Current State

**Baseline:** Uptime `main` at `225d524d34905b700b128faafeaad3584333c2bf`, October 22, 2026. Connections is the active Uptime gameplay experiment. It is not a migration of Packet or a declaration that Packet has been replaced. For exact behavior, inspect `Assets/Scenes/Connections.unity`, its serialized references, `Assets/Scripts/Connections/`, and `Assets/Prefabs/Connections/` on the working branch.

## Playable slice and simulation

The scene has an authored four-node, three-physical-link topology: EXT1–API, API–DB, and API–AUTH. A **logical connection** has a stable short ID and a route through that graph. Physical links and logical paths have separate view responsibilities. The scene's serialized `runMode` is **Generated**; Authored Witness mode retains c1/c2/c3 for focused review and tests.

`SimulationHost` owns one fixed-step simulation clock, active connection state, inspection history, and resolution. `ConnectionTrafficDirector` uses its own seeded random stream to queue concrete spawn plans and register arrivals through the host; queued plans can also be supplied explicitly. The default generated mix now draws from [six ecommerce purpose templates](connections-purpose-anomaly-generation-v0.md) on the existing ambient scheduler. Each purpose samples normal LOAD and WORK independently, then may become benign, a LOADER, or a DELIVERY attempt with a recorded anomaly. Inspector weight overrides tune the purpose mix without copying built-in operations. The earlier ten profiles have zero default ambient weight and remain available through explicit `spawn <profileId>` and witnesses; a weight override can re-enable one. Rates and cadence are playtest tuning, not balanced game rules. Completed or killed generated connections leave active ownership but remain inspectable for that run. The [traffic document](connections-arcade-traffic-v0.md) preserves prior phase provenance.

The current allocator grants **target-node LOAD** proportionally when requests exceed capacity after baseline LOAD. Transit nodes and physical links are route geometry in this model; they are not CPU/disk/memory/link-bandwidth allocation constraints. One allocated LOAD advances one current-operation WORK unit per second. An operation may be READ, WRITE, or EXECUTE and have its own WORK and desired LOAD. Authored multi-operation arcs advance to the next operation on the following fixed step, retaining the connection ID, route, target, and path; only final completion resolves the connection. See [LOAD](connections-arcade-load-v1.md) and [operation arcs](connections-arcade-operation-arcs-v1.md). The current arcs do not change target or route between operations.

Presentation consumes simulation state. Node LOAD bars show requested LOAD / capacity with capped fill and threshold colors; they do not separately show allocated LOAD, though the simulation tracks requested and allocated values independently. Overloaded color is not a threat label. Path pulses express WRITE toward the target, READ toward origin, or EXECUTE locally, while speed/satisfaction treatments reflect allocation. The terminal has structured inspect output with an operation timeline and retains keyboard input. `inspect`/`i` default to [compact cards](connections-inspect-v2.md) with ID, purpose, timeline, and current effective requested LOAD badge; `-v`/`--verbose` adds performance status and any contention reason, WORK, activity, and ETA. Any number of IDs can be inspected in one command, including retained completed or killed history. Inspect cards have a full-height identity rail and a large, identity-colored ID pill at upper left; the purpose, operation timeline, and LOAD pill sit to its right, with verbose details below. The old horizontal TitleBar behind the purpose is removed, and verbose status omits the numeric ALLOC fraction. The ID, operation, and LOAD pill borders use a rounded-rectangle antialiasing material derived from the line-rendering coverage approach. `kill|k <id> [id ...]` independently attempts each unique ID in order, reporting failures without stopping later kills; successful kills release LOAD and retain the existing Health penalty and recorder effects; `demandtest` queues a deliberate high-demand mechanical witness. Development-only `spawn <profileId>` queues any configured profile, including `basic-threat`, `delayed-threat`, `spiking-threat`, `spiking-benign`, and `overload-demo`, independently of ambient generation. Inspection and command entry are immediate; consequences progress in simulation time. [Terminal and inspect cleanup Editor captures](../Review/ConnectionsTerminalInspectCleanup/README.md) show compact and verbose cards, multi-ID results, and a completed connection at 16:9. They are Editor previews, not Play Mode screenshots.

The following Threats slice descriptions record how each capability entered the prototype. Their manual-only status was temporarily superseded by Generated Hostile Traffic V0; Purpose & Anomaly Generation V0 returned those fixed profiles to manual-only defaults while retaining their definitions.

[Threats V0 Slice 1](connections-threats-v0.md) originally added one manual-only hostile profile: a 12-LOAD WRITE with 240 fixed WORK against DB. Its operation-completion effect emitted an infection-delivery occurrence without persistent state at that milestone. The later [Infections V0 Slice 1](connections-infections-v0.md) attaches a typed Phantom LOAD payload to concrete plans and applies it on that same completion path. `kill` before completion prevents delivery; a kill after delivery leaves the node infection in place. Hostile intent remains internal and is not inferred from demand or revealed by ordinary inspect. Cleaning, Level Director, and source blocking remain unimplemented.

Threats V0 Slice 2 added manual-only `delayed-threat`: an ordinary 2-LOAD, 16-WORK READ followed on the next simulation step by `basic-threat`'s 12-LOAD, 240-WORK WRITE. Only normal completion of the WRITE emits its one delivery occurrence, at operation index 1. The arc keeps its ID, route, path, and target; inspection and pulse direction follow the current operation. Killing during either operation prevents pending delivery. Both fixed threat profiles are available for manual spawning.

[Slice 3A's behavior foundation and Slice 3B's LOAD keywords](connections-behavior-foundation-v0.md) treat the existing logical definition as a **Connection Plan**, distinct from a scheduled `ConnectionSpawnPlan`. A connection-owned runtime executes optional connection-scope and operation-scope behaviors before allocation each fixed step. Active modifiers add to base WORK and requested LOAD; inspection, presentation, allocation, and recorder snapshots read the resulting effective values. Modifier changes append attributable per-connection history and recorder events. OVERLOAD adds one sustained LOAD delta after a delay; SPIKING alternates normal and elevated demand with independent timers per connection. Originally manual-only `spiking-threat`, `spiking-benign`, and `overload-demo` profiles exercise those behaviors through `spawn <profileId>`; at that slice, existing profiles and ambient weights were unchanged. Connection-scope WORK adjustment, dynamic plan edits, and player-facing modifier logs remain deferred.

Logical paths now use [route-aware lane ordering](route-aware-lanes-v1.md) across occupied physical links. The presentation solver favors continuous paths, grouped onward routes, geometric branching, and stable prior positions. It retains the current per-link width and pill packing rules; the path view animates changed offsets.

Generated connections use [randomized lowercase letter-digit IDs and six rotating identity accents](identity-presentation-v1.md). The ID pool is deterministic per traffic seed and separate from traffic random draws; explicit authored IDs remain valid. Active at-speed paths and identity pill outlines carry the assigned accent, while slowed and stalled paths retain warning colors. Active Inspect cards share the accent and completed history stays muted.

Active paths now show [one identity-colored terminal marker](terminal-markers-v1.md) where their final lane meets the target-node border. Transit nodes have no marker. Its size follows the beam width and arrival angle, with prefab padding; it tracks lane reflow, appears after establishment reaches the target, and fades with path departure. This is presentation of the existing target-node LOAD model.

`ConnectionsRunRecorder` automatically starts a new JSON recording on Play start, saves the same file every 30 seconds of real elapsed time, and saves on Play stop. Its profile metadata stores effective ambient weights and purpose-generation rates. Every `plan_queued` event records the full concrete plan before spawn; purpose-generated plans also record the internal decision branch, selected keyword locations, anomaly IDs, and normal/final operation values. This distinguishes intentional subtle delivery and benign outliers without exposing them in ordinary inspection. Snapshots include authoritative Network Health values, rates, counts, and failure state, and legitimate-kill penalties are attributable events. `mark <note>` records a bookmark with an immediate snapshot and saves; `log save`, `log dir`, and Editor-only `log folder` expose the file. Editor logs go to `<project-root>/Logs/UptimeRuns/`, which Git ignores. This is observational run evidence, **not** a game save, score ledger, replay engine, or source of random traffic. See the [recorder document](connections-run-recorder-v0.md) and its [example JSON](examples/connections-run-recorder-example.json).

[Infections V0 Slice 1](connections-infections-v0.md) adds simulation-owned Phantom LOAD on target nodes. A completed DELIVERY operation applies its queued typed payload once; the infection survives carrier retirement, draws real LOAD after baseline and before connection allocation, and is cleared on reset. The existing node LOAD bar shows the unexplained total demand. Developer-only `spawn <purposeId> obj:delivery|loader` overrides select an objective without ambient hostile/delivery rolls; DELIVERY accepts `inf:phantom-load` and `infp:load=N`. Direct `infect`, `uninfect`, and `infections` controls support mechanical testing. The recorder stores forced decisions, concrete payloads, infection events, and resource breakdowns. Ordinary inspection does not reveal an infection label. Cleaning and other infection kinds remain deferred.

[Network Health V0](connections-network-health-v0.md) is a Connections-only, live service instrument. A fixed deadline per connection comes from the authored operation plan at activation. The simulation applies substantial LOAD-shortfall or escalating overdue drain to legitimate active service, passive recovery only when no degradation is present, and one immediate penalty for killing a legitimate connection. Slowed and overdue observations include all active connections, while hostile traffic causes no direct Health penalty. Zero latches failure without ending the run. Lifecycle service facts remain with each connection for future evaluation. A compact clock/Health row with signed rate sits above the terminal; TAB expands the same instrument to show aggregate conditions, peak requested/capacity node LOAD, and recent events while keeping the terminal and topology visible. Terminal history stays bottom-anchored above the persistent prompt on resize. The display-only 24-hour clock has configurable shift bounds and simulation-time mapping; shift progression and Performance scoring are not implemented. The model and values are provisional.

## Authority and provenance

The four current phase documents linked above retain detailed contracts and witness cases. Their older “future/deferred” lists were written at different points; where a later phase has merged, this current-state document and the final runtime/scene take precedence. In particular, the original [Connections V0 brief](../historical/connections-v0.md) is historical phase context. [Network and Connection Grammar V1](../historical/network-connection-grammar-v1.md) preserves useful conceptual distinctions but maps to an earlier CPU/disk/memory/link resource model and one-stage baseline. Those implementation claims were superseded by the LOAD, arc, traffic, and recorder merges. Do not use it as the current allocator or scene contract.

Connections does not yet implement cleaning, further infection kinds, company contracts, hacker campaigns, full run progression, scoring, upgrades, or network growth. Those remain relevant **Uptime-wide design directions** in [Uptime Design](../Design/Uptime_Design.md); their precise Connections mechanics are not yet established. Nor should the Packet simulation's tick, scan, or score rules be imported implicitly.
