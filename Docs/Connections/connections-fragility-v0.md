## Service contract

The host owns service enforcement separately from Network Health and Integrity. Both canonical scenes serialize `serviceTimeoutsEnabled: true` and independent `serviceTimeoutTolerance: 1.5`. The host default is off for historical scenes and old component fixtures. Tolerance is provisional playtest tuning.

On arrival at the destination, nominal duration is the sum of every authored operation's WORK / LOAD. Budget is nominal duration × tolerance; deadline is establishment time + budget. Pending, queued and Connecting time do not consume it. Operation changes, modifiers, natural LOAD variation, allocations and Overclock never move it. Health reuses the same nominal-duration helper but retains its separate tuning/deadline and existing penalties; it does not enforce service timeouts.

The existing host loop splits segments at legitimate deadlines, including coarse manual steps. Normal allocator completion/effects settle first; a final operation completing on the deadline succeeds. An unfinished connection then resolves `TimedOut` with `ServiceTimeout` cause. An intermediate operation completing there remains completed, but the remaining connection times out. Existing next-outer-step operation transition holds remain unchanged: coarse calls do not invent intermediate operation starts. The allocator and its existing completion tolerance are unchanged.

Timeout stores effective required WORK before cleanup, ends connection behaviors, clears natural demand, releases LOAD and refreshes allocation, then retires through the existing red failure departure. It cannot advance more WORK or deliver unfinished completion effects. Historical state retains operation index, partial WORK, completed-operation count, timing, resolution and cause.

## Business failure receipts

`SimulationHost.Resolve` claims each canonical state once before accounting or callbacks. Every non-Completed legitimate resolution adds one immutable `BusinessFailure` receipt: canonical ID, resolution/cause, time, operation index, partial/effective required WORK, one unit and cumulative total. This includes player kills during Connecting/Active, Blackout, isolation, timeout and any other legitimate interruption using that seam. Hostiles and successful completions add zero. Receipt ownership never depends on recording, aliases, display refreshes or callback delivery. Reset clears the ledger; unresolved/frozen work stays unresolved. Completed earlier operations are retained.

There is no failure allowance, firing rule, new ending, shift duration/quota, WORK ledger, importance weighting or final shift/run accounting policy. CORE semantics remain unchanged. The counter is a cumulative count since simulation reset.

## Danger and temporary presentation

The host refreshes a deterministic projection from model state. For a processing operation, estimate remaining effective WORK / actual productive rate; positive remaining WORK at zero rate means infinity. Future operations use authored nominal WORK / LOAD. A held transition estimates its not-yet-started operation nominally. `AtRisk` means projected service time exceeds remaining budget (using the existing small numerical tolerance). This is a current-conditions estimate, not a guarantee: future modifiers/competition and transition waiting can change it. Overclock expiry is not forecast. Risk can clear; current performance can be slowed yet safe, or at speed yet endangered after earlier delay.

Existing network identity pills show a small authored `AT RISK` label inside their unchanged packing bounds without changing identity colors, path meshes, rhythm, lanes or routing. Verbose inspect adds an authored service block, distinct from current-operation ETA: remaining service time, fixed budget and a current-rate-plus-nominal-future explanation. Resolved cards and collapsed rows say `TIMED OUT`. A scene-authored label in the permanent strip shows `BUSINESS FAILURES N`. These are temporary treatments for playtesting, deliberately easy to replace.

**Hostile compatibility:** The identical nominal target, countdown and projection are presented for every established connection, with no `IsHostile` test or enforcement flag in presentation. Hostiles retain their previous lifetimes: target expiry does not terminate them; LOADER withdrawal, INFECTOR delivery, BACKDOOR rates and other effects remain existing authority. A hostile can remain active with zero displayed service time and projected risk. The target is therefore a service expectation, not a promise that every connection disappears there. This avoids an immediate classification tell from missing countdowns or danger tags, but prolonged survival beyond the target may become an investigative clue. Resolving that residual asymmetry is an open design question; attack timing was not retuned to hide it.

## Recorder

New recordings use schema 3, adding `TimedOut`/`ServiceTimeout`. Metadata includes enforcement configuration/tolerance. Establishment/resolution receipts retain nominal duration, actual budget and fixed deadline; resolution also includes termination required WORK and completed-operation count alongside existing timestamps, partial WORK and full definition. One `business_failure` event per failure includes cause, one unit and cumulative total; network snapshots retain the total. Connection snapshots retain remaining time, projected seconds (`-1` for infinity), deadline and risk. No per-frame event stream is added.

## Controlled benign fixture and provenance

The Play Mode-only Editor menu **Tools → Connections → Load Benign Fragility Fixture** uses the real `product-lookup` PURPOSE builder, existing queue, lifecycle and allocator. It temporarily disables automatic/hostile generation, fixes seed 1701, disables natural noise/outliers and WORK spread, and sets the existing READ to 100 WORK / 5 LOAD. DB capacity remains10; baseline 4 and two simultaneous requests yield 3 LOAD each. All changes are in-memory test configuration; stop/reload to discard them. Canonical ambient scheduling, templates and tuning are unchanged.

One request completes after 20 service seconds within a 30-second budget. Two competing requests need 33⅓ seconds and time out at 30 with 90 WORK each. At about10 service seconds, Overclock DB raises capacity to 15 (11 after baseline), grants 5 each and saves both. Killing one then sacrifices one business connection and saves its peer. Baseline2 instead grants 4 each: slowed service completes after 25 seconds and stays safe. Total requested LOAD14 never crosses the canonical severe-overload threshold 15; these fixtures avoid Integrity damage overtaking business failure. Both canonical scenes exercise the same model despite different ingress lengths.

These are controlled mechanical witnesses, not evidence of naturally balanced ambient pressure. [PR #125](https://github.com/abeering/uptime-game/pull/125) found actual-population feedback suppresses demand and matched-volume independent arrivals alone provide little sustained pressure. [PR #126](https://github.com/abeering/uptime-game/pull/126) found more useful, seed-sensitive contention with moderate workload and PURPOSE concentration; excessive LOAD can damage Integrity first. Neither experimental scheduler is imported. [#115](https://github.com/abeering/uptime-game/pull/115) and [#124](https://github.com/abeering/uptime-game/pull/124) provided references for host-owned exact-once accounting; this implementation adapts that ownership pattern at connection resolution, without their WORK credit, quotas, clocks or endings. No commits were cherry-picked and no experimental PR was modified.
